02 / Engineering depth

I focus on the engineering between components.

Explore how different parts of a system fit together—from distributed learning and data pipelines to application architecture and the boundary between software and hardware. Each view highlights interfaces, transformations, validation points, and design trade-offs.

01 / BOUNDARIES

Separate responsibilities

Separate ingestion, transformation, domain logic, persistence, and presentation into clear responsibilities. Assemble dependencies through an explicit composition point so that components can be tested and changed without tightly coupling the entire application.

02 / DATA INTEGRITY

Make transformations explicit

Treat data reconciliation as a defined engineering stage: map source schemas, normalise identifiers, specify deduplication rules, and make the distinction between full and incremental loads explicit.

03 / TESTABILITY

Test invariants, not only examples

Test system invariants as well as individual examples. Examples include equivalent feasible solution sets, stable data transformations, repeatable outputs, and metrics that remain consistent under equivalent conditions.

04 / MEASUREMENT

Design fair comparisons

Control experimental conditions, use paired seeds where appropriate, select statistical tests that match the design, and correct for multiple comparisons. Reliable conclusions depend on the quality of the evaluation protocol.

05 / OPERATIONS

Build for real usage

Design for operation as well as implementation: provide clear command-line workflows, structured logs, explicit failure states, repeatable data refreshes, and documentation that supports the next person responsible for the system.

06 / TRADE-OFFS

Choose the simplest sound tool

Establish a strong baseline before introducing additional complexity. Prefer the simplest method that meets the requirements, and justify more sophisticated techniques with measurable improvements.

Case study · Multi-agent RL

Adaptive traffic signal control

A controlled comparison of independent and centralised-critic policy optimisation in a shared traffic simulation, designed to isolate the contribution of critic architecture.

System design

  • Built a SUMO/TraCI environment with nine signalised junctions under simultaneous control and 211 routes.
  • Designed a 20-dimensional observation and a continuous green-duration action space from 5 to 120 seconds.
  • Implemented IPPO and MAPPO in PyTorch, sharing the actor, observation, reward, hyperparameters, and interaction budget to isolate the critic difference.
  • Designed a multi-term reward around queue and delay, throughput, progress, starvation, and spillback.

Evaluation and engineering

  • Compared policies over 100 paired seeds, discarding a 300-second warm-up.
  • Used paired statistical tests with Holm correction across nine network metrics.
  • Fine-tuned the selected policy on two OpenStreetMap-derived Damascus networks: Kafr Sousa and Abu Rummaneh–Muhajireen.
  • Delivered approximately 6,800 lines of Python across 55 modules, one CLI for training and evaluation workflows, structured JSON logging, and 17 pytest suites.
Reported comparison in the CV: MAPPO mean wait of 82.9 s versus 85.1 s for IPPO, with 6,125 vehicles cleared (+1.9% over IPPO). These are results from the stated experimental setup, not a universal traffic-control claim.
Case study · Data platform

MTNSYR telecom data warehouse

Transform two differently structured telecom datasets into a consistent analytical model that supports reporting, customer segmentation, and customer-value analysis.

Pipeline stages

  • Ingestion: work with source data from MTN Syria and Syriatel.
  • Reconciliation: map differing columns and normalise city names into shared representations.
  • Data quality: deduplicate records at phone level and support both incremental and full reloads.
  • Modelling: load a five-table SQLite star schema for analytical queries.
  • Analytics: train a Random Forest customer-lifetime-value regressor and a class-balanced decision tree for four spending segments.
  • Delivery: expose the workflow through a single CLI and a nine-page Streamlit / Plotly dashboard.
The project uses a layered architecture and a dependency-injection factory to compose the components. It was developed by a team of two.
